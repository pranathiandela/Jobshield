SCANNING: FAKE CHEQUE
Lost$0

The cheque that paid for a laptop

Marcus, 26, junior front-end developer

Marcus aced a text-only interview for a $45 an hour remote role. A few days later, "HR" posted him a cashier's cheque for $5,400 and told him to buy a laptop and desk from their approved vendor.

The money showed up in his banking app, so he sent $4,850 to the vendor. Three days later the cheque bounced and the bank took the full amount back.

  • The interview happened only by text, never on video.
  • The employer asked him to send money by transfer.
  • A cheque can show in your balance days before it is verified.

The "easy tasks" that kept costing more

Priya, 31, switching careers

A WhatsApp message offered $200 a day to "boost app ratings". Priya paid in $100 of crypto on day one and withdrew $180, which was enough to make her trust the site.

Then came a "bonus task" that needed a $3,000 deposit to release her $8,000 balance. More deposits followed, then a $10,000 "tax fee". After that the site vanished.

  • An unsolicited offer from a personal phone number.
  • She had to deposit her own money, in crypto, to unlock earnings.
  • A small early payout was used to build trust before the big ask.
SCANNING: ID THEFT
Fraudulent loans$0

The onboarding portal that wasn't

David, 48, operations specialist

David applied for a remote healthcare coordinator job. The link led to a copy of the company's portal, and two hours after a short questionnaire he had an offer.

"Onboarding" asked for his full SSN, a scan of his licence, bank details and his mother's maiden name. Two months later he found two car loans taken out in his name.

  • The address was careers-biohealth-inc.com, not the real company domain.
  • ID documents were requested before any live meeting.
  • The offer arrived two hours after applying.

Four messages. Decide if each one is a real offer or a scam.

Can you spot the fake address?

Scammers copy the brand and change the web address. Look closely.

biohealth.com/careers real
careers-biohealth-inc.com/apply lookalike

Ask yourself six questions

If an offer fails even one, stop and verify before you reply.

?

Does it ask for money?

Real employers never charge for training, equipment, visas or "registration".

?

Did they contact you first?

Unrequested offers on WhatsApp or Telegram are a classic opening.

?

Does the email match?

Check that the sender domain is the official one, not a lookalike.

?

Is there a live interview?

No video call and a very fast offer are both warning signs.

?

Is the pay too good?

High pay for very simple work is bait.

?

Do they want ID early?

Never share ID numbers or bank details before a verified, signed contract.

What if it already happened?

Act in the first 24 hours. Speed matters most.

Call your bank

Ask for a stop or recall on any transfer and lock affected cards and accounts.

Protect your identity

If you shared ID details, place a credit freeze and change every password the scammer could know.

Report it

Keep all messages and receipts. In India, call 1930 or use cybercrime.gov.in. In the US, report at reportfraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov.
